# Biological Literature & Baselines

## 1. Eisenia fetida (Compost Worms) Environmental Limits
- **Temperature:** Optimal range is 15°C to 25°C. Mortality begins rapidly at sustained temperatures > 35°C.
- **Moisture:** Optimal moisture content is 70% to 80%. Levels < 50% cause desiccation; > 85% cause anaerobic conditions (leading to ammonia spikes and worm death).
- **Oxygen:** Highly aerobic. Requires continuous gas exchange.

## 2. Compost Maturity and Respirometry
- **Biological Principle:** As easily biodegradable organic matter is consumed by earthworms and microorganisms, overall microbial activity declines.
- **CO2 Evolution Rate:** A highly active (immature) compost produces large amounts of CO2. Mature compost reaches a stable, low respiration baseline (e.g., < 2.4 mg CO2-C/g OM/day).
- **Slope as an Indicator:** Because absolute CO2 accumulation in an unsealed bin is difficult to measure perfectly, we measure the *rate of change* (slope) of CO2 accumulation during a sealed period. As the compost matures, the peak slope flattens, forming an asymptote.

## 3. Thermal Gradients
- Active decomposition (thermophilic phase) generates internal heat. A healthy vermicompost bed should maintain a stable internal temperature without sharp localized hotspots. 
- A vertical gradient helps identify if anaerobic pockets are forming near the bottom of the bin (P3 depth).

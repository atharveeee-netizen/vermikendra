2.3 Data flow
1. The node wakes on its timer, or on a LIS3DH interrupt when the lid moves.
2. It switches on sensor power (3V3_S), reads all sensors, and switches sensor power off.
3. It builds the payload, signs it, and transmits.
4. It opens a 2-second receive window for the acknowledgement and any command.
5. The gateway's radio service checks the signature and sequence number, decodes the packet and
publishes JSON to MQTT.
6. The ingest service stores the reading. The engine computes calibrated values, events, respiration
rate, heat forecast and readiness, and evaluates alert rules.
7. The API pushes updates to open dashboards over WebSocket. The notifier sounds the buzzer and
sends Telegram messages when online.
8. Commands (misting, configuration) travel back through MQTT to the radio service and ride on the
next acknowledgement.
2.4 Design principles
Offline first. Every P0 feature works with no internet.
Safety at the edge. The node protects the bed on its own if the gateway is down.
Portable gateway. The gateway software runs on any Linux machine with the LoRa radio attached
over USB, so a laptop can stand in for the CM4.
Trends over absolute claims. Respiration and gas readings are used as trends against the bin's
own history, not as laboratory measurements.
Replicable. Commodity parts, open source, documented calibration.

3.5 Lid pod and respiration hardware
A small IP65 box fixed under the lid. Its side openings are covered with ePTFE membrane, which
lets air and gas through but not liquid water.
The SCD41 and BME688 face the headspace through the membrane. The LIS3DH is fixed rigidly to
the lid.
A 40 mm fan in a lid vent pushes headspace air out during the flush phase. Inlet holes (about four
holes of 6 mm) are covered with fine mesh to keep flies out.
During measurement the fan is off. The small inlet holes keep leakage low and similar from cycle to
cycle, so the respiration trend is reliable even though the absolute value is approximate.
The SCD41 is rated for non-condensing conditions. The membrane and flush reduce condensation.
The firmware flags the pod if relative humidity stays above 95% for more than 12 hours.

4.2 HAT compatibility (risk R2)
The Waveshare SX1262 868M LoRa HAT is a UART "wireless serial port" module. It runs its own
firmware, is configured through M0 and M1 jumpers and register commands, and may add its own
packet framing. The RAK4631 drives its SX1262 directly. Interoperability is therefore not guaranteed
and must be proven first.
Spike test (days 1 to 3)
1. Configure the HAT, following the Waveshare wiki, for transparent mode on a fixed channel in the
865 to 867 MHz range, lowest air data rate, and no encryption key.
2. On the RAK4631, run a RadioLib receiver that sweeps SF7 to SF12, bandwidths 125 and 250 kHz,
and sync words 0x12 and 0x34, printing every packet received. Transmit repeatedly from the HAT.
3. Once packets arrive, lock the parameters, test the reverse direction, and document any framing
bytes the HAT adds.
4. If no reliable two-way link exists by day 3, switch to a fallback.
Fallbacks, in order
F1. A second RAK4631 on a WisBlock Base, connected by USB, as the gateway's radio modem.
The same radio runs at both ends, so compatibility is guaranteed. About ₹4,600.
F2. An SPI SX1262 module driven directly from the gateway.

4.4 Security
Each node has a 32-byte HMAC key, set at flashing and stored in the gateway configuration. Keys
are never committed to the repository.
The 4-byte tag stops casual spoofing and corruption. Payloads are not encrypted because the data
is not sensitive.
Replay protection: the gateway rejects a sequence number already seen for that node within a
window of 256. The node saves its sequence number to flash every 50 packets and adds 50 on
boot, so it never repeats after a reset.
Downlink commands carry a tag too, so a node ignores commands not signed with its key.
4.5 Reliability
The gateway acknowledges every uplink. The node retries up to 2 times with a random 1 to 3 s
delay, then stores the record (P1).
Store-and-forward (P1): a ring buffer of 2,000 records, about 7 days at 5-minute sampling, in
internal flash. After a successful acknowledgement the node sends up to 5 buffered records per

7.2 Probe calibration
Each probe i gets an offset from the three-point water bath in section 8.1. If residuals exceed 0.2°C, a
gain is fitted as well.
T_cal[i] = gain[i] * T_raw[i] + offset[i]
7.3 Heat-risk forecast
For each probe, fit a least-squares line to the last 30 minutes of readings (at least 5 points), giving the
current temperature T and slope s in °C per hour.
if T >= T_crit:                     critical (HEAT_NOW)
elif s > 0.3:
    t_cross = (T_crit - T) / s      # hours until the probe reaches T_crit
    if t_cross <= 2:                critical (HEAT_FORECAST)
    elif t_cross <= 6:              warning  (HEAT_FORECAST)
The alert clears when T is at least 1.5°C below T_crit and s is 0 or less for 30 minutes.
7.4 Moisture
1. Two-point calibration per sensor in the real bedding: raw_dry (air-dried bedding) and raw_wet
(saturated, drained for 10 minutes). Capacitive sensor output falls as moisture rises.
2. Wetness index: w = (raw_dry - raw) / (raw_dry - raw_wet) , from 0 (dry) to 1 (saturated).
3. Map w to gravimetric moisture content with a linear fit from 4 to 6 bedding samples, each weighed
wet, dried to constant weight, and weighed again. Moisture content (wet basis) = (wet mass - dry
mass) / wet mass x 100.
The thresholds in 7.1 refer to this moisture content.
7.5 Respiration rate
For each cycle, using SCD41 readings every 5 s during the measure phase:
1. Discard the first 60 s, while air settles after the fan stops, and any reading of 4,800 ppm or more.
2. Fit a straight line C(t) = a + s * t to the remaining points (up to 480 s). The slope s is in ppm
per minute.
3. The run is valid if it has at least 20 points and R² of at least 0.90. Otherwise it is flagged low quality.
4. Convert to a mass-normalised index, in mg of CO2-carbon per kg of bed per hour:
n_air   = P * (V_head / 1000) / (8.314 * T_K)          # mol of air in headspace
R_index = s * 1e-6 * n_air * 12.01 * 1000 * 60 / m_bed
  P       headspace pressure, Pa (from BME688)
  V_head  headspace volume, litres (entered at setup, updated at harvest)
  T_K     headspace temperature, kelvin
  m_bed   bed mass, kg = platform mass - bin tare
5. Optional normalisation to 25°C, assuming respiration doubles every 10°C: R_25 = R_index * 2
** ((25 - T_bed) / 10) .
Because of leakage the index is lower than the true rate. It is used only as a trend within the same bin.
